## Bridge — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** A holds a sustained gluteal bridge: gluteus maximus,
hamstrings, and lumbar erectors all fire isometrically; the upper trapezius
and posterior deltoid stabilise the shoulder base. Expect noticeable
glute fatigue at ~60–90 s for untrained athletes.

**Pelvic alignment.** A's pelvis is lifted ~25 cm off the floor with the
hips in extension and the spine in a neutral-to-slightly-extended arch
(lumbar extension ~25°). The geometric effect is to raise the contact
axis to the level of B's kneeling pelvis without B having to flex deeply.

**Common faults for A.**
- Lumbar hyperextension to gain more height → moves the load from glutes
  to lumbar erectors; spinal compression rises. Squeeze glutes instead.
- Pelvic asymmetry (one hip lower) → SI-joint torque. Both feet symmetric,
  both glutes engaged equally.
- Neck pushed into the floor → keep a neutral cervical spine; eyes up.

**Common faults for B.**
- Sitting back on the heels → contact axis drops. Stay upright kneeling.
- Trying to lower onto A's elevated pelvis with B's lumbar in flexion →
  B's spine takes B's weight. Hinge from B's hips, keep B's spine long.

**Stamina note.** This is an isometric strength pose for A; treat it as
a 30-60-second interval rather than a sustained position.
