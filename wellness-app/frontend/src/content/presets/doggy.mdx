## Doggy (rear-entry, quadruped) — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** Partner A bears trunk weight through the shoulder girdle
(serratus anterior + rotator cuff) and wrists; partner B works through hip
extensors (gluteals, hamstrings) and a constant low-grade lumbar erector
contraction to hold the pelvis level.

**Pelvic alignment.** A's lumbar curvature is the principal angle-tuning
parameter: ~10° of anterior pelvic tilt ("cat-cow" extension) maximises
geometric reach and posterior-wall contact; a flat or posteriorly tilted
pelvis collapses the angle.

**Common faults.**
- A's wrists hyperextend → switch to forearms ("leapfrog" variant) or
  closed fists.
- A's shoulders shrug up to the ears → drop the scapulae, lengthen the
  back of the neck.
- B leans the trunk forward and loses pelvic level → the resulting hip
  flexion shortens functional reach and increases A's spinal load. Keep B's
  trunk vertical.

**Skip / modify when.** A has carpal tunnel symptoms, acute lumbar
disc pain (flexion-sensitive), or shoulder impingement.
