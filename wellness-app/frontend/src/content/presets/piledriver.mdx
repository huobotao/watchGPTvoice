## Inverted shoulder-stand — risk notice + kinesiology

> ⚠️ **Risk grade: high.** Included here as the geometric extreme of
> pelvic elevation, *not* as a recommended position. Cervical-spine
> injury risk is non-trivial. Skip unless you are explicitly trained in
> inversions (gymnastics, aerial, yoga shoulder-stand) and have a
> partner who can release the position immediately.

**Loaded chains.** A is in a partial shoulder-stand: cervical-spine
**compression** with the full body mass above; the only structural
defenders are the upper trapezius and the cervical extensors held
isometrically. Brachial-plexus compression symptoms (arm tingling, hand
numbness) appear within ~30 s for many people. B is standing or kneeling
above and is in a near-neutral position.

**Pelvic alignment.** A's pelvis is raised to ~standing pelvic height,
inverted. The geometric pay-off is the steepest possible contact angle —
which can be achieved more safely by **butterfly** (edge-supine, pelvis
on a wedge) without inversion.

**Substitution.** If the appeal is steep angle: use butterfly. If the
appeal is novelty: try amazon (deep-squat over) instead, which loads
B's quads but doesn't compress A's neck.

**Hard contraindications.** Any cervical-spine pathology, hypertension,
glaucoma, recent eye surgery, pregnancy, history of vertebral artery
dissection or transient ischemic episodes.

**Safety protocol if attempting anyway.**
- Set a 30-second hard time limit.
- B never adds downward force.
- Pre-agreed signal (verbal or tap) for immediate release.
- Land A by rolling out backward over the shoulders, not by collapsing
  the legs sideways (lateral neck-loading is the worst failure mode).
