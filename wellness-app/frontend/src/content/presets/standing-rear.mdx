## Standing rear (braced) — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** A is in a 60° hinge with hands braced on a surface —
the lumbar erectors, hamstrings, and gluteals all fire to hold the
position eccentrically; the wrists and shoulders bear A's upper-body
weight if the surface is at the wrong height. B is in a quarter-squat
with quadriceps and adductors doing isometric stabilisation.

**Pelvic alignment.** Match heights. The single most important geometric
parameter is the *vertical offset* between A's pelvis and B's: if A
is much taller, A must bend deeper or stand on a lower surface. If A
is shorter, B should widen the stance to lower B's pelvis. Mismatched
heights force compensatory lumbar flexion in whoever is taller, which
is the leading cause of post-event back soreness in this configuration.

**Brace surface choice.**
- Counter / table at hip height → cleanest geometry, supports trunk.
- Wall → only the hands support, so A's lumbar erectors carry the full
  trunk weight. Tiring fast.
- Bed (soft) → unstable, demands more A core. Avoid for V1.

**Common faults.**
- B holds A's hips and pulls back → axial overload on A's lumbar; B
  should drive forward instead.
- A locks the knees → posterior knee strain. Keep ~10° flex.
- A drops the head between the arms (cervical flexion + shoulder
  protraction) → impinges the rotator cuff. Eyes 30° forward, scapulae
  packed down.

**Skip when.** Acute lumbar pain, B has knee instability, or A has
shoulder impingement.
