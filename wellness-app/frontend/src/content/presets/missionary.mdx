## Missionary — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** Partner A's posterior chain rests passively; the lumbar
spine bears partial weight from B and benefits from a small pillow under the
sacrum to neutralise pelvic tilt. Partner B's principal load is the upper
extremity (anterior deltoid, triceps) and the abdominal column, both of which
act isometrically to hold a plank-like trunk position.

**Pelvic alignment.** Both pelvises pitch ~25–30° anteriorly from horizontal.
This shallow angle is the geometric reason missionary is reported as
low-stimulation for many anatomies — anterior wall contact is incidental
unless the **CAT** ("coital alignment technique") variation is used, in which
B shifts ~5 cm cranially and both partners rock rather than thrust.

**Common faults.**
- B locks the elbows. → Keep a 30° elbow flex; the joint loads ligamentously
  when hyperextended.
- A holds the head off the surface. → Neck flexors fatigue in 30 s; either
  rest the head or use a pillow.
- A flares the lumbar instead of tilting the pelvis. → Posterior tilt
  (5–10° lumbar flexion) reduces sacroiliac shear.

**Skip / modify when.** Late-trimester pregnancy (supine vena-cava
compression), recent abdominal surgery, or active SI-joint pain.
