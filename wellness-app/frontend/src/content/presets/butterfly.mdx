## Butterfly (edge supine, pelvis elevated) — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** A is supine with the pelvis elevated and supported by
the surface edge — there's *no* postural muscular load on A; the work is
done by gravity holding the position. B is standing or kneeling and
supports A's lifted legs through the upper extremities (shoulder anterior
deltoid + biceps holding A's calf or thigh).

**Pelvic alignment.** A's elevated pelvis brings the contact axis to B's
standing pelvic height, eliminating the need for B to squat or kneel
deeply. The result is the cleanest "match-heights" geometry in the
library: A's spine stays neutral, B's spine stays neutral, neither
partner is doing antigravity work.

**Why edge-of-bed matters.** Without the edge, A's lifted legs put A's
lumbar into flexion under load (cf. folded deck-chair). With the edge,
A's sacrum is supported and the legs hang freely off A's hips — the load
moves out of the lumbar.

**Common faults.**
- B raises A's hips by lifting the legs aggressively at the knee →
  knee-extension torque on A. Hold A's thighs near the hip, not the calf.
- A lets the pelvis sag off the edge → spine bridges unsupported.
  Slide A back so the sacrum is on the surface, only the gluteals
  hang.
- B braces by leaning on A's thighs into deep hip flexion → defeats the
  purpose; B's contact angle is set by B's stance width, not by pushing
  A's legs closer.

**Indications.** Good geometry for hip-mobility-limited A, hand-arthritic
A (no upper-body work), or when A wants minimum exertion.
