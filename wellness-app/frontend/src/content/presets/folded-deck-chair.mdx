## Folded (deck-chair) — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** A is in deep passive hip flexion (~110°) with the knees
pulled toward the chest — the loaded structure is the lumbar disc, which
is now in flexion under compressive load. B holds a controlled forward
lean on the knees with hip flexors and hamstrings working eccentrically
to resist gravity.

**Pelvic alignment.** A's pelvis posteriorly tilts as the knees come up;
this opens the pelvic angle to its maximum, shortening the functional
distance between pelvises and giving B the deepest reach in the library.

**Range-of-motion prerequisites for A.**
- Hamstring length sufficient for ~110° straight-leg-raise (not the case
  for ~40% of adults; substitute a less-deep variant if so).
- Lumbar tolerant of loaded flexion. If A has any history of disc-related
  pain, **substitute butterfly or missionary** instead.

**Common faults.**
- A pulls the knees with the hands and over-rounds the lumbar →
  reduce knee-to-chest distance; keep the lumbar long.
- B drops weight forward through straight elbows onto A's shins →
  shin pressure; keep B's forearms on the bed beside A's shoulders.
- A grips the legs against gravity isometrically → cramps. Use the hands
  to hold the back of the thigh, not the calf.

**Skip when.** Lumbar disc pathology, hip impingement, hip-replacement
post-op (deep flexion is generally contraindicated).
