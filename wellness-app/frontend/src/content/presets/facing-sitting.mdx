## Face-to-face seated — kinesiology notes

**Loaded chains.** Both partners hold upright trunk against gravity through
the lumbar and thoracic erector spinae plus the deep core (transverse
abdominis). Hip flexors stay short throughout. It looks restful but is
isometrically demanding — expect erector fatigue at ~3–5 minutes for
untrained partners.

**Pelvic alignment.** Pelvises sit at the same height and directly oppose
each other along a vertical contact axis — a clean geometry for full
anterior body contact, eye-line sharing, and synchronous breathing.
Motion is generated by both partners rocking the pelvis (lumbar
flex/extend ±10°) rather than by linear thrust.

**Common faults.**
- Slumping into thoracic flexion → loss of contact angle and breathing
  restriction. Lengthen through the crown of the head.
- B perched only on A's thighs without weight on the sacrum → unstable
  contact; sit B's sacrum into the cradle between A's femurs.
- Embrace pulled too tight, locking ribcage expansion → relax shoulders
  down and back, allow breath to broaden the ribs.

**Indications.** Couples building synchrony, trauma-sensitive contexts,
post-cardiac-event recovery (low MET cost, eye contact, gentle motion).
